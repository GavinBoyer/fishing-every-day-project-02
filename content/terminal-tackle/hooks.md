# Hooks

### The Point of it All

Without the hook, catching a fish would be virtually impossible. The first fishing hooks were originally sharp gorges meant to wedge in the fish's mouth when it bites. There were some J hook resemblance, but not near what we have in the modern day. The hook holds your bait and ultimately the fish while you are reeling it in. You can use essentially any hook and [rod](rods/index.md) combination, granted they are of relative size.

### Types of Hooks

There are several different types of hooks, all with different uses:
- **Bait Hooks**: Meant to hold your bait on the hook via small barbs on the shank. Often used for bait fish.
- **J Hooks**: The name is derived from the shape of the hook. These have a straight shank with a curved hook point. You must set the hook when using these or you will miss hook up opportunities.
- **Circle Hooks**: Circle hooks look like J hook except they starts to bend sooner and the hook point comes to, what appears to be, a G shape. You do not have to set these hooks and they will settle in the corner of the fish's mouth
- **Octopus Hooks**: Similar to bait hooks except with a wider gap and shorter shank. The eye is turned away from the hookpoint to make it parallel with the [line](fishing-line). Often used for smaller species.
- **Treble Hooks**: Treble hooks are banned in many states because of its triple hook point on a single shank. While they do increase your hookup ratio, it is extremely damaging to the fish. It is extremely illegal to use trebles on protected fish.
- **Siwash Hooks**: Siwash hooks have a long shank with a straight eye. While they resemble traditional hooks, their straight eyes allow lures to correctly sit.
- **Worm Hooks**: These has a bend near the eye to hold the bait in place. These are used for rigging worms or [soft plastics](artificial-baits.md)
