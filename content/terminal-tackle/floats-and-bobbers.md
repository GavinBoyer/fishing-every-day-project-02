# Floats and Bobbers

### What's the Difference?

The terms "float" and "bobber" are used interchangeable. The former coming from the UK and bobber originating in North America. There is no true difference between the two but we will use both to mean: a buoyant device to use as an bite indicator while suspending the bait.

### Float Systems

There are only two systems in which floats operate; a fixed system and a slip system.
- **Fixed System**: The bobber stays clips directly onto the line and does not move. Best for shallow water as it gets awkward to cast when it is set for deep water. These floats are best for panfish.
- **Slip System**: The bobber can slide freely in between the hook and a bobber stop. These are excellent for any depth as it is very easy to adjust the bobber stop, and it goes through the [rod](rods/index.md) guides allowing for easy casting. Best used for walleye, jigging for crappie, and catifish in deep water.

### How to Use Bobbers

When using a bobber, you want to avoid [fluorocarbon](fluorocarbon.md) because it sinks and can pull your float under. [Braid](braid.md) and [mono](monofilament.md) are the best options for float fishing. Floats are excellent for rivers as they left your [bait](organic-baits) drift down the river and get in front of more fish faces. Thus, increasing your bite ratio. While fishing with a float, you are waiting for it to disappear under the surface, this means a fish has your [hook](hooks.md).
