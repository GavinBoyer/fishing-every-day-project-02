### The Elegant Option

Fly reels are an extremely specialized piece of equipment specifically made for fly fishing. Fly fishing is using the weight of the line, instead of the weight of the lure, to cast the fly out, and the reel is a key part in keeping [fly rods](fly-rods.md) balanced.

>What are the different types of fishing reels and which one should be used by a beginner like me when starting out in freshwater fly-fishing? For a beginner in freshwater fly fishing (e.g., for trout or panfish), a standard arbor fly reel with a simple click-and-pawl or basic disc drag system, matched to the weight of their fly rod (e.g., a 4-5wt rod with a 4/5wt reel), is ideal. #### Key Features:

- **Line Storage**: The reels main function is primarily to store the fishing line, backing, and leader.
- **Drag System**: These systems can range from a small click-and-prawl to extremely sophistcated sealed disc designs.
- **Balance**: An integral part of the long [fly rod's](fly-rods.md) balance.
- **Retrieve**: Fish are often stripping in by hand, but the reel is frequently used for larger fish and storing extra line.

#### How to Cast a Fly Reel

Casting a fly reel is nowhere near as simple as that of a [spincast](spincast-reels.md) or [spinning reel](spinning-reels.md). You must create momentum with the weight line through a series of whip-like motions to cast the virtually weightless fly into the water. First you have to strip the amount of line you want to be cast. Then, start your whipping motions to get momentum behind the line. Once you have enough line in the air, you can let the fly hit the water and wait for a bite. To retrieve, on lighter fish, you want to strip them in by hand, and move over to the reel for larger, harder fighting fish.
