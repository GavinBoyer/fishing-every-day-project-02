# Spinning Reels

![Diagram of a spinning reel](https://fisherreel.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/Parts-of-spinning-reel.png)

### The Versatile Option

Spinning reels are, without a doubt, the most popular and most versatile [reels](reels/index.md) on the market. They are used on anything from little ultralight [rods](spinning-rods.md) to the massive 15' [surf rods](surf-rods.md). They can be used in saltwater and freshwater and can catch species from small panfish to several hundred pound tuna.

#### Key Features:

- **Open-Faced Spool**: The spool is exposed and makes managing line much easier.
- **Ease of Use**: Fairly easy to learn. Beginners can still learn them while they maintain the capability for experienced anglers.
- **Versatility**: Excellent for finesse fishing with light lures, but larger varieties can handle true giants and bait.
- **Bail Arm**: Instead of a button like on [spincast](spincast-reels.md) and [baitcasters](baitcasting-reels.md), spinning reels have a thin wire arm that holds the line in place. Once it is flipped open, the line can flow freely.

#### How to Cast a Spinning Reel

Casting a spinning reel is fairly straight-forward. First you grab the line near the [reel](reels/index.md) with your index finger. You then open the bail with your free hand. Swing the rod from behind your head and release the line from your finger as the rod moves to the target. Once there is enough line out, you can close the bail and retrieve using the handle.
