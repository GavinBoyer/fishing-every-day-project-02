### The Precision Option

Baitcasters are many angler's favorite reel, particularly bass anglers. This is because of their excellent precision, control, and power. These characteristics are needed, especially when fishing with heavier lures and line. While baitcasters do have excellent casting distance and precision, they can also become quite a mess with their "bird's nests."

![Bird's nest or a tangle in a baitcasting reel](https://coastalanglermag.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/LINE-TANGLE.jpg)

Baitcasters offer more direct line control (using the thumb on the spool), better handling of heavy lines/lures, potentially longer casting distance with heavier setups, and often more robust drag systems and gears in a compact package. #### Key Features:

- **Revolving Spool**: Instead of a stationary spool like a [spinning reel](spinning-reels.md), the spool rotates during the cast to loose line. This makes thumb control a necessity to avoid "backlashes" and "bird's nests."
- **Advanced Control**: Gives the user the most precision out of any [reel type](reels/index.md).
- **Power and Strength**: Baitcasters are designed to use heavier line and target larger, harder fighting fish such as bass or pike.
- **Braking System**: They contain a braking system and spool tension knob to help perfect casts. They change the spools rotational speed and requires adjustment for separate lures.
- **Two Styles**: Low-profile reels contain less line and are popular among bass anglers. Round reels contain larger amounts of line used for heavier applications.

#### How to Cast a Baitcasting Reel

Before you even think about casting, you need to adjust your tension knob and braking system to match your lures weight. To do this, you want to tighten the tension knob all the way and click the thumb button. Slowly loosen the knob until the lure slowly falls to the ground without a backlash. 

To cast, first, you press the thumb bar, much like a [spincast](spincast-reels.md), making sure to maintain pressure on the spool. Then swing the rod smoothly from behind your head and gradually reduce the pressure applied from your thumb as the lure travels. Apple more pressure as the lure hits the water to avoid a backlash and turn the handle to engage the retrieve mechanism.
