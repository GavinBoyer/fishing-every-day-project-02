# Conventional Reels

![Diagram of a conventional reel](https://images.contentstack.io/v3/assets/blt964243cdd7810dea/bltce8d7a45355e5ac8/638f53538ea5221b4e20b5fb/anatomy-of-saltwater-conventional-reels.jpg)

### The Big Game Option

Conventional reels are primarily used as trolling reels behind boats. These reels are constructed specifically for deep-sea fishing. They easily battle extremely large fish species such as marlin, tuna, and sailfish. There are also a few large freshwater species targeted with these like giant catfish and sturgeon. 

>What type of fishing reel can be used to catch an alligator or crocodile? While not typical angling targets, very heavy-duty conventional reels spooled with extremely strong line would be the closest fishing gear equivalent for such an extreme task, though specialized capture equipment is usually employed. #### Key Features:

- **Robust and Durable Construction**: These reels are normally made from strong, durable materials like machined aluminum or graphite composites.
- **High Line Capacity**: They can hold up to ==thousands== of yards of extremely heavy fishing line, usually [braid](braid.md).
- **Powerful Drag Systems**: Since these reels are battling such large fish, they need to have sophisticated drag settings to handle that caliber of animal.
- **Gear Ratios**: These reels often have low gear ratios meaning you can really put some power behind the cranks. However, there are some two-speed models that offer both low and high-speed retrieve.
- **Level-Wind Mechanism**: Though not all models have this, some feature a level-wind mechanism that evenly distributes the line across the reel so you don't have to guide it with your thumb.

#### How to Cast a Conventional Reel

Traditionally, long distance casting is not very common with conventional reels, unless on a [surf rod](surf-rods.md). Most of the time you will just drop the bait out of the back of the boat and troll along with it until something bites, then the fight is on.
