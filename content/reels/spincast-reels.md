# Spincast Reels

![Diagram of a spincast reel](https://kayaksview.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/The-Anatomy-of-A-Spincast-Reel-Parts.jpg)

### The Beginners Option

Spincast reels are the perfect option for beginner anglers. Not only are they simple to use, but because they are a closed-faced reel, and unlike [baitcasting reels](baitcasting-reels.md) they hardly tangle. Spincasts are by far the easiest reel to use. Experienced anglers also enjoy spincasts for their ease of use. Particularly catfish anglers.

Spincasts are excellent for virtually any light fishing scenario. Especially for beginners and those that want to fish without having to worry about untangling line every cast. The only major disadvantages of spincasts are their shorter casting distance and accuracy, less robust drag systems, and lower quality components compared to that of other [reel types](reels/index.md). These rods typically come pre-spooled with [monofilament](monofilament.md).

#### Key Features:

- **Ease of Use**: Extremely beginner friendly with the simple push-button mechanism.
- **Budget-Friendly**: By far the most affordable reel on the market.
- **Closed-Faced Reel**: The spool is covered by a metal or plastic sheathing, protecting it from the elements and minimizing line tangles, however, it can make clearing the tangles that do happen a little more challenging.
- **Fixed-Spool**: The button on the reel controls whether the spool is fixed (reeling) or free (casting).
#### How to Use a Spincast

Spincasts are extremely simple. You simply push and hold the button on the front of the reel, swing your rod from behind your head (as if throwing a ball), and release the button when the rod tip is pointing at your intended target. You can also stop mid-cast by pushing the button again. To retrieve you turn the handle in its respective direction.
