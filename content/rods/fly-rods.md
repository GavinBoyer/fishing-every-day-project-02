# Fly Rods

### What is a Fly Rod?

A fly rod is meant for a specific type of fishing called fly fishing. In fly fishing you are using the weight and momentum or your line to cast instead of pushing a button or releasing a line. Fly rods are controversial within the fishing community with a lot of fly fisherman calling fishing with [[spinning-rods]] "cheating." Fly rods tend to be much longer, skinnier, and lighter than most other rods with some [[surf-rods]] being the exception. Fly rods use a fly reel which has no gear ratio and essentially just acts as storage for your line instead of an actual reel.

>To understand a fly rod, you must first understand the physics of the cast. In traditional fishing, using [[spincast-rods]] or [[baitcasting-rods]], the rod acts as a lever to throw a heavy object, like a lure or a baited hook. The fishing line is usually thin, light, and follows the weight of the lure through the air.
>	
>Fly fishing reverses this dynamic. An artificial fly is often made of just a few feathers, some fur, and a hook. It weighs almost nothing. You cannot "throw" a feather thirty feet using a standard fishing rod. Instead, the fly rod is designed to cast a thick, coated fly line. The line itself provides the weight needed to load the rod.

#### Significant Features of Fly Rods

- **Reel Placement and Seat**: On a fly rod, the reel is placed on the underside of the rod. Where the reel sits is called the seat. The seat consists of a threaded barrel with rings to lock the reel in place. Most of the time, the seat is made of wood or plastic to reduce the weight, but on saltwater rods, they tend to be anodized aluminum to aid against corrosion.
- **Guides**: There are two different kinds of guides on a fly rod. Stripping Guides and Snake Guides
	 1. **Stripping Guides**: These guides are located closest to the handle and are often larger and ceramic lined or have a stone insert. They have a more durable design to resist the friction of pulling the line by hand or "stripping."
	 2. **Snake Guides**: These guides run the rest of the rod. They are constructed of thin wire to help the thick fly line from not sticking when it is wet.
- **Weight and Action**: Fly rods use slightly different measurements when determining weight of the line and rod action. Fly rods come in a variety of weights, this is not the actual weight of the rod rather the weight of the line possible to throw on the rod. The line weight ranges from 1wt to 10wt. 1wt being used for small trout and panfish and 10wt being used for big game like sharks, tarpon, or other deep-sea species. The action for fly rods is comparable to that of other rods with the action ranging from fast to slow. Fast being only the rip of the rod bends, and slow being it bends closer to the handle.
- **Construction Materials**: Much like other rods, fly rods tend to be made from graphite, which is the most popular option, and fiberglass. Fly rods are also made out of bamboo which is the traditional material. The bamboo rods, while extravagant, are not as durable or practical.
